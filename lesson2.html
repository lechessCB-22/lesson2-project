Early Life

Born on 22 December 1887 in Erode, British-ruled India, Srinivasa Ramanujan was a self-taught mathematical prodigy raised in a traditional Tamil Brahmin Iyengar family in Kumbakonam. Despite showing extraordinary early genius—mastering advanced trigonometry by age 13, independently developing complex theorems, and studying G. S. Carr's A Synopsis of Elementary Results in Pure and Applied Mathematics—he struggled with formal education, failing non-mathematical subjects and dropping out of college. After years of living in extreme poverty while pursuing independent research, he finally gained local recognition in Madras around 1910 through the Indian Mathematical Society, which set the stage for his groundbreaking work.
